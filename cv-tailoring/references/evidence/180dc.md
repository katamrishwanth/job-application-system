### Scope
Main deliverable: a communications and donor strategy addressing declining
funding revenue. Key questions the project addressed:
- What communication/outreach channels does HI Belgium use, and how
  effective are they at reach, engagement, and donor conversion?
- Which donor segments show the biggest opportunity?
- What targeted communication methods can expand into those segments?

### Methodology (from the scoping deck)
Status Quo Analysis (desk research, strategy map, SWOT/PESTEL) → 
Segmentation Analysis & Expansion Strategy, run over an 8-10 week roadmap
with a kick-off, mid-cycle SteerCo presentation, and final recommendation.

### Client findings (from meeting notes, for Rishwanth's own reference)
- Current pain points: inefficient digital engagement, low ROI from
  street fundraising (poor retention of younger donors, 30-35), a
  regular-donor base skewing elderly (65-70)
- Target opportunity: the 40-45 age group for better long-term retention
- Potential to double the "major giving" segment from €0.5M to €1M
- Average donation sizes: €50 one-off, €12/month regular, €50-100k major gifts
- B2B side relies on relationships with foundations/corporations; strong
  French and Dutch donor connections, negligible German presence

## Engagement B — ESCP carbon footprint (the one previously documented)

**CLIENT CONFIDENTIALITY:** the client was **ESCP Business School itself**.
Do NOT name the client. Describe as "a European higher education institution"
or "a multi-campus European institution".

## The brief

Find carbon footprint reduction partnerships for students across 5 European
campuses: Paris, Berlin, London, Madrid, Turin.

## Carbon footprint mapping

Identified **4 categories** of student carbon footprint:
Mobility (inter-campus travel) | Food | Commute | Others

Built an **impact/effort matrix** to prioritise. **Top 3 recommended:**
1. Train vouchers / green transport funding
2. Plant-based food options on campus
3. Bicycle / e-scooter subscriptions

## Mobility — route economics (THE HARD NUMBERS)

Benchmarked plane vs. train on cost and time across 3 inter-campus routes:

| Route | Train vs. plane cost | Train vs. plane time | Verdict |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Paris–London** | 12.46% cheaper | 52% faster | Highest-ROI green travel shift |
| **Paris–Turin** | 3.72% more expensive | Comparable | Good opportunity |
| **Paris–Berlin** | 29.61% more expensive | 60% slower | Hard to incentivise |

- Identified campus-specific intra-semester travel issues (e.g. M1 students
  flying from Berlin/Turin/Madrid to Brussels for seminars)
- Proposed savings of **€60/student** on the Madrid route, **92g CO2 offset
  per student**
- Travel partners researched: SNCF, Deutsche Bahn, Mollow, Byway, Staze,
  BlaBlaCar Daily

## Food — campus-specific recommendations
- **Paris:** HappyCow partnership + gamification app with sustainability food rating
- **Berlin:** Veganz partnership (vegan supermarket chain), Lieferservice.de discount
- **London:** Plant Based News content partnership, Broadway Vegan Market monthly stalls
- **Madrid:** Madrid Vegan Community, Viva Burger student discounts
- **Turin:** Torino Vegano partnership, vegan food drives

## Commute — campus-specific recommendations
- **Paris:** electric bike accommodation improvements, Mobeelity incentivisation
  platform, Smovengo bike-sharing
- **Turin:** BlaBlaCar Daily carpooling for EMBA students from Milan, class
  clustering to reduce travel frequency, Mollow/Byway for plane-free travel
- **Madrid:** BiciMAD bike rental, Meep multi-modal app, Ciclogreen rewards
  platform, electric shuttle bus from city centre using Agora housing data
- **London:** JouleBug and Wespire gamification, National Rail Card awareness campaign
- **Berlin:** Nextbike (60 min free per ride across Germany), BVG Berlin-Abo
  subscription (€29/month)

## Additional opportunity identified
- **Greenly** (carbon accounting platform) offered to donate their source code
  to a research project. Identified as a partnership opportunity. 180DC had
  2 previous projects with Greenly in 2020/2021.

## Methodology
- Primary research: qualitative interviews with companies and stakeholders
- Secondary research: price comparison data, emissions data, transport timetables
- Frameworks: impact/effort matrix, cost-benefit analysis per route
